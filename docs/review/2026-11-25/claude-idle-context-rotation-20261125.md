## A large session left alone continues in a new one before its cache is cold (lane/claude-idle-context-rotation-20261125)

R221. Until now a Claude member of 180,000 tokens or more was handed over for its size only before its next item, and the butler never. One that nobody used for an hour went cold or was compacted by the CLI itself (2.1.332: at 0.9 of the cache lifetime, seven times in seven days, 53.6-53.9 minutes after the last request), and whoever wrote to it next paid for that.

Now the once-a-minute check of quiet sessions has one more reason. A Claude member, or the designated butler, that is at least 180,000 tokens, still warm (under 0.9 of the lifetime its last request was billed at), at least an hour old and idle for 45 minutes is handed over like any other quiet session. Idle by two clocks, the later of which decides: the last activity NAW saw of it, and the time of its last request in its own record. Before 45 minutes the size is no reason at all: nothing is kept as pending, the butler's wakes are not held, no stall is told, and the other reasons keep their own quiet (10 minutes for memory, 2 for the butler's compactions, 30 otherwise). With a five-minute cache it never applies. The limits, the read of the size again just before the handoff starts, one rotation at a time and every gate (draft, turn, question, stored message, background work, timer, team, quiet window, the person's recent use, decisions that cannot be handed over, an earlier rotation) are the ones already there; none is new.

Outside that check: a rotation for size that the person interrupts, and an old session that waits for the person after its handoff, now wait 45 minutes of the person's quiet instead of 30, also when it was started before an item (`personQuiet`). The handoff request, the space notice, the log, the butler's designation and the re-read already knew this reason. `wakeSteward` and the stall notices are unchanged.

Held automatic messages are kept by the sender's backend and the dispatcher does not see them. Three kinds. One held because the recipient was at work, or for its minute, is tried again within a minute while the recipient is idle, and one that goes in makes it busy, which the read before the request sees: a session idle for 45 minutes has none. One held for a migration window of the recipient's backend waits for as long as the window is open, whether or not the recipient is idle, and is looked at once a minute: a window that closes early leaves it held for up to a minute that no gate shows. So for two minutes after a window closes (two of those looks) the size is no reason for its sessions: `view` marks them from the windows NAW has recorded as closed, or that are past their deadline, also after NAW starts again, and the once-a-minute check finds no reason in the size, as before the 45 minutes: nothing is kept as pending and nothing is told. Only the size waits: the long conversation, the compactions and the memory, and the rotation before an item, are as they were. One staged in a turn of its sender that ends after the old session has is refused as not sent (`recipientIssue`), as with every other reason for a rotation.

Limits. The check runs only while a window is open, once a minute with nothing queued, as before. The window is nine minutes, so a session that cannot be asked in it (a draft, the one rotation slot taken) goes cold and is left; nothing is told of that. The CLI's own compaction at 0.9 of the hour falls in the last half minute of that window: found when the rotation would start, there is none, then or later; while the request is already on its way in, the handoff is written after the compaction and the rotation goes on once. That order was tested with a stand-in backend, not with a real CLI left alone for 54 minutes: the first real quiet rotations after the install show it. A window that closes in the last two minutes of the nine leaves its sessions to go cold. Not tested: a machine asleep through the minute in which a held message would be tried again; the two minutes can pass in that sleep, and the message then ends as not sent, which its sender and the butler are told. The butler is asked only if nothing woke it for 45 minutes.
