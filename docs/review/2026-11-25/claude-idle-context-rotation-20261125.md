## A large session left alone continues in a new one before its cache is cold (lane/claude-idle-context-rotation-20261125)

R221. Until now a Claude member of 180,000 tokens or more was handed over for its size only before its next item, and the butler never. One that nobody used for an hour went cold or was compacted by the CLI itself (2.1.332: at 0.9 of the cache lifetime, seven times in seven days, 53.6-53.9 minutes after the last request), and whoever wrote to it next paid for that.

Now the once-a-minute check of quiet sessions has one more reason. A Claude member, or the designated butler, that is at least 180,000 tokens, still warm (under 0.9 of the lifetime its last request was billed at), at least an hour old and idle for 45 minutes is handed over like any other quiet session. Idle by two clocks, the later of which decides: the last activity NAW saw of it, and the time of its last request in its own record. Before 45 minutes the size is no reason at all: nothing is kept as pending, the butler's wakes are not held, no stall is told, and the other reasons keep their own quiet (10 minutes for memory, 2 for the butler's compactions, 30 otherwise). With a five-minute cache it never applies. The limits, the read of the size again just before the handoff starts, one rotation at a time and every gate (draft, turn, question, stored message, background work, timer, team, quiet window, the person's recent use, decisions that cannot be handed over, an earlier rotation) are the ones already there; none is new.

Outside that check: a rotation for size that the person interrupts, and an old session that waits for the person after its handoff, now wait 45 minutes of the person's quiet instead of 30, also when it was started before an item (`personQuiet`). The handoff request, the space notice, the log, the butler's designation and the re-read already knew this reason. `wakeSteward` and the stall notices are unchanged.

Held automatic messages are kept by the sender's backend and the dispatcher does not see them. A session idle for 45 minutes has none held for being at work or for its minute: each is tried again within a minute while it is idle, and one that goes in makes it busy, which the read before the request sees. One staged in a turn of its sender that ends after the old session has is refused as not sent (`recipientIssue`), as with every other reason for a rotation.

Tests: 22 new ones. On the earlier code (6b629684) 17 fail: 16 of the 17 in `queue-dispatch.test.cjs` and the butler's event in `steward-rotate-before-wake.test.cjs`; the five that pass there say what must not change (before 45 minutes nothing is held or told; a large butler idle for 0, 10, 31 and 43 minutes is woken within its first minute). 30 edits that break these rules: 29 fail at least one test; the other, any pending reason holding the butler's wake, changes nothing for this reason, which is never pending. One test's comment is corrected. `steward-rotate-before-wake.test.cjs` no longer samples this machine's processes.

Limits. The check runs only while a window is open, once a minute with nothing queued, as before. The window is nine minutes, so a session that cannot be asked in it (a draft, the one rotation slot taken) goes cold and is left; nothing is told of that. The CLI's own compaction at 0.9 of the hour falls in the last half minute of that window: found when the rotation would start, there is none, then or later; while the request is already on its way in, the handoff is written after the compaction and the rotation goes on once. That order was tested with a stand-in backend, not with a real CLI left alone for 54 minutes: the first real quiet rotations after the install show it. The butler is asked only if nothing woke it for 45 minutes.
